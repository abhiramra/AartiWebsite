Milestones

Six children in a rented house, and everything that came after

1991

The First Step

A rented house, and six abandoned children in it.

1996

Aarti Home is Born

Thirty-six children by now, and the rented house had run out of room. We moved to a building of our own and called it Aarti Home.

1999

First Livelihood Programs

Our first vocational and livelihood training, and the first time we tried to get local women earning.

2000

Aarti Women's Centre

Opened the Aarti Women's Centre, somewhere a woman in trouble could go without needing a reason.

2007

Global Recognition

Our advocacy work appeared in the BBC documentary India's Missing Girls.

2008

Aarti English Medium School

Founded the Aarti English Medium School, so the children in our care had somewhere decent to be taught.

2009

School Expansion

Moved the school into a building of its own and started taking day students from the surrounding area.

2009

Lalitha Women's Cooperative

Women trained at the Women's Centre set up the Lalitha Women's Cooperative, a tailoring collective they ran and earned from themselves.

2012

Childline Partnership

Appointed regional nodal organisation for the 1098 Childline, which meant the district's emergency calls about children started coming to us.

2014

Moving to Aarti Village

Moved the children to Aarti Village, 2.5 acres of green campus built as cottages rather than dormitories.

2015

Mana Bidda Project

Launched Mana Bidda, a three-year campaign across the district against female foeticide and infanticide.

2018

Abhaya Project

Started Abhaya, a three-year project to protect the local women who had made themselves targets by doing this work.

2020

COVID-19 Crisis Relief

Ran COVID-19 relief for more than 70,000 families and put oxygen plants into local government hospitals that had none.

2022

A Permanent Home for the Women's Centre

The Women's Centre moved into a building of its own. Open to any woman, and home to the Lalitha cooperative and several other ventures.

2024

Children For Children After-School Pilot

An intensive after-school mathematics programme for government school students, run through Children For Children. It went better than anyone had planned for, which is what made the case for putting the teaching in the neighbourhoods themselves.

2026

Aarti Slum Education Centres

The pilot became ten Aarti Slum Education Centres. Hundreds of children now have a lit room and a teacher at the end of the school day, close enough to walk to.
