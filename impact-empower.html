Impact on the community

Our first livelihood training program began in 2000 — but we soon saw that skills alone weren’t enough. The women who came to us were often ostracised by their communities and held back from any hope of financial independence.

So we built something fuller: a program that combines motivation, skill development, opportunity, community, and — above all — confidence. Two decades on, the results speak for themselves, and the numbers keep growing.

Meet the women of Aarti

Our story is woven from theirs — women whose courage, strength, and resilience have shaped everything we do.

“My story started when my father sold my textbooks to buy notebooks for my brother. Uneducated and dependent on my husband, I wanted to do something for myself. When I heard Aarti was starting a tailoring program, I enrolled.

There was a big backlash — as I came home with ₹50 a day, people started asking if I was selling myself. Sandhyamma came to our basti and spoke with the community elders. I persisted while many who joined with me dropped out, and I soon became a good tailor and joined the collective.”

— Nilofer, Aarti Women’s Centre

Our programs

Our focus is on building income-generating skills — and everything that has to surround them: confidence, rights, money sense, mental health, and a community to fall back on.

🧵

Skill Building

Hands-on training for women with little or no formal education — tailoring and fashion design, beautician skills, embroidery, handicrafts and toy-making, fabric painting, and block printing, taught by our own master trainers.

📋

Employability

For educated women and students stepping into the workforce: communication and social skills, résumé writing, interview practice, and the foundations of a strong work ethic.

🤝

Soft Skills

Many of the women who reach us have spent their lives inside the house, with little say in anything beyond it. These sessions cover what nobody taught them — how to speak to an employer, handle a bank counter, read a bus route — so that the world outside feels navigable rather than frightening.

🖥️

Computer Literacy

Comfort with a keyboard, a mouse, and the internet. Women learn to use a computer and a smartphone for the things that matter to them: filling forms, searching for work, staying in touch, and managing money online without depending on anyone else.

💰

Financial Literacy

Earning money and keeping hold of it are two different problems. Women learn to budget, save, operate a bank account, and recognise a predatory loan — so that what they earn stays theirs, rather than being signed away to a husband, a relative, or a moneylender.

⚖️

Legal Awareness

Most women who walk through our doors have never been told what the law owes them. These workshops set out their rights plainly: the protections that exist, the laws they can lean on, and exactly what to do — and who to go to — if they face violence or abuse.

💚

Psychological Workshops

Years of hardship leave marks that training alone cannot reach. Working alongside counsellors, these sessions give women a safe place to name what they are carrying — anxiety, depression, the aftermath of abuse — and to begin working through it without shame.

🔄

Gender Sensitisation

We run workshops in schools across Kadapa district for students of both genders. The exercise that lands hardest is Reverse Roles, where children act out the parts traditionally assigned to the opposite gender — an afternoon that does more than a term of lectures on equality.

🌟

Women Empowerment Program

In partnership with the Women Entrepreneurship Platform (WEP), our WEP centres build the skills and confidence young women need to lead social change in their own communities.
