Our Story

A Child on the Street in Kadapa

It was a hot summer day in Kadapa in 1992 when our founder, Sandhya Puchalapalli, found a two-year-old girl abandoned on the street — left to starve after her father had killed her mother. Sandhya could not leave her there. Nor, once she had begun asking, could she ignore how many other children in the district were in much the same position. Together with a handful of others who felt as she did, she set out to establish a home for abandoned children and for those in need of protection.

The initiative, officially registered as the Vijay Foundation Trust, began in a rented house with just six children.

How Aarti Home Got Its Name

By 1996, the family had grown to 36 children, and they needed more space. They moved into a new building and named it Aarti Home, after Sandhya's niece — a girl who had been the organisation's first donor, and who died at eighteen.

Why the Work Kept Widening

As the number of children in our care grew, so did our understanding of the societal forces putting girls onto the streets in the first place — and it became clear that shelter, which answered the emergency, did nothing whatever about the causes of it.

Our growth over the last three decades has been largely unplanned, in the sense that each part of the work began as a response to a need we were already seeing in the community. More than 30 years later, what started as a rented house for six children has become an umbrella organisation working across the care, education and advancement of women and children.

Somewhere in the course of it, the work stopped being a project and became a way of going about things: helping girls and women find their own footing, and their own say in what happens to them.

Our Vision

We envision a world in which every girl is wanted, educated, and free to determine the course of her own life.

Our Mission

Our work rests on four pillars.

Care

Providing a home for abandoned and orphaned girls, alongside the support families need to keep their own daughters.

Educate

Educating children properly, and sustaining that education with community support outside the classroom.

Empower

Building women's independence through vocational training, sustainable livelihoods, and networks they can rely on.

Advocate

Speaking for those with no one else to speak for them, campaigning against female foeticide, and pressing the case for girls' inherent rights.
