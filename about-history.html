Our Story

Kadapa, 1992

On a hot summer day in 1992, Sandhya Puchalapalli found a two-year-old girl abandoned on a street in Kadapa. The girl's father had killed her mother and left her there to starve. Sandhya could not walk away from it, and she did not much want to do it alone either. She and a handful of other people set about making somewhere for children like her to go.

The initiative, officially registered as the Vijay Foundation Trust, began in a humble rented space with just six children.

Where the name came from

By 1996, the family had grown to 36 children, and they urgently needed more space. They moved into a new building and called it Aarti Home, after Sandhya's niece. Aarti had been the trust's first donor. She died at eighteen.

How it grew

The more children arrived, the harder it was to ignore why they kept arriving. Shelter dealt with the consequence. It did nothing about the cause.

Very little about the last three decades was planned in advance. Each part of the work started because something in front of us needed doing. Thirty-odd years on, what began as a rented house with six children runs as an umbrella organisation for women and children across the district.

Somewhere along the way it stopped being a project and became a way of working.

Our Vision

A world where a girl is wanted, schooled, and left to decide her own life.

Our Mission

The work falls into four parts.

Care

A home for abandoned and orphaned girls, and support for the families trying to keep their daughters.

Educate

Schooling children properly, and backing them up outside the classroom.

Empower

Training women to earn a living, and making sure they have other women to lean on.

Advocate

Campaigning against female foeticide, and arguing for girls’ rights wherever it needs arguing.
