Education is a right, not a privilege.

We started Aarti School in 2007 because the children in our care needed somewhere decent to go, and so did a lot of children who were not in our care.

Sponsor a Child’s Education

Four ways we go about it

A school of our own. After-school centres in the neighbourhoods children actually live in. Money and mentoring for girls who would otherwise be pulled out at sixteen. And a network of young volunteers who fund and teach a good deal of it themselves.

Aarti School

Nurturing bright minds

Aarti School does well in the state board examinations, which we mention because English-medium schools that admit first-generation learners are often assumed not to. Children who start late, or start behind, go through a Bridge Program until they can hold their own in class. A fair number of them end up arguing the case for girls' education better than we do.

Aarti Slum Education Centres

Aarti Vidhya Vikas Kendra — learning where children live

In the slums around Kadapa, a child comes home from school to one shared room. No desk, nowhere quiet, and nobody in the house who can help with the homework. School on its own was never going to be enough.

So we put the room in the neighbourhood instead. Aarti Vidhya Vikas Kendra runs ten after-school centres now. Each is a lit, quiet room with a teacher who knows every child in it by name. Children turn up at the end of the school day and work on the subjects that tend to decide whether they carry on at all.

Grades have climbed term on term. But what the teachers keep mentioning is that children who used to sit silently at the back now put their hands up.

Aarti Rising Stars

Empowering future leaders

Aarti Rising Stars looks for girls doing well at school whose families are about to stop paying for it after Grade 10. Rather than send money and hope, we bring them in.

They live at Aarti Home and go to school with our own children. Same routines, same study hours in the evening, same expectations. That last part seems to matter as much as the fees do. A girl who was the only one still studying in her house is suddenly one of many.

It started with 3 girls in 2024. There are 21 now.

Children for Children

Young people investing in young people

Children for Children came out of the COVID-19 pandemic. When schools shut, more than 70 student volunteers in 17 cities across India, the US and Dubai organised themselves, fed 632 children, made teaching materials and ran classes online.

The crisis passed and the volunteers stayed, which is when the programme found the work it was actually good at. It funds merit scholarships for bright children whose families cannot, and runs the after-school tutoring that turns a bare pass into a mark worth having.

The premise has not changed since 2020. Given something concrete to do, young people show up for each other.
