At Aarti, education comes first

Three ways we help children learn

We believe education is a fundamental right. We deliver it through three connected programs — a school of our own, support that keeps promising girls in the classroom, and a global community of children helping children.

Program 1

Aarti School

Nurturing bright minds

Our English-medium school welcomes children who might otherwise miss out — including first-generation learners and those joining school later than most. A dedicated Bridge Program gives extra academic and English support so no child is left behind, and students grow into confident advocates for girls’ education.

Program 2

Aarti Cares

Empowering future leaders

Aarti Cares finds high-achieving girls from disadvantaged families who are at risk of dropping out after Grade 10, and gives them the mentorship, resources, and encouragement to keep going. What began with just 3 girls in 2024 now supports 21.

Program 3

Children for Children

Kids helping kids, across borders

This program connects young volunteers around the world with Aarti’s children through online sessions and shared projects. During the COVID-19 pandemic, over 70 student volunteers from 17 cities across India, the US, and Dubai came together to provide meals for 632 children, build learning materials, and run online classes.

Inside Aarti School

How we teach

🏠

Know Your Child

Teachers and social workers visit children’s homes to understand each family’s situation and plan for the child’s growth. Since 2020 it has lifted attendance, parental involvement, and wellbeing.

🔧

Makerspace

In partnership with BiBox Labs, students in Grades 4–6 build real electronic circuits. The hands-on learning has boosted attendance, confidence, and problem-solving skills.

✏️

Creative Schooling

First-time learners build age-appropriate reading, writing, and arithmetic through the Aarti Creative School — most catch up to their grade level within six months.

A student’s story

Kadar Amma: strength, and breaking the cycle

When her father, Satram, passed away during the pandemic, Kadar Amma and her ailing mother were left with no means of support. Already an Aarti School student, she was brought to Aarti Village under our COVID-affected family program.

Through her education and leadership classes — and the support of the Women’s Education Project — a girl who had known mostly hardship has grown into a confident young leader, determined to build a brighter future for herself.
